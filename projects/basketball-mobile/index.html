BASKETBALL MOBILE

An Android multiplayer basketball title featuring full-court 5v5 matches, fast-paced 3v3 half-court sessions, gesture-based touch swipe shooting, and Epic Online Services networking.

Role Mobile & Gameplay Developer
Engine / Tools Unreal Engine 5 · EOS · Android NDK
Target Platform Android (Mobile)
Year 2025

Gameplay Demonstration Reel

Project Overview & Architecture

Basketball Mobile was developed to deliver responsive arcade sports gameplay on mobile touchscreens without sacrificing the depth of physics-driven shooting. Players can compete in an official 15-minute full-court match or quick 3v3 half-court street ball showdowns.

The project integrates Epic Online Services (EOS) for peer-to-peer and dedicated session matchmaking, mobile touch gesture input recognition, and optimized collision meshes for rapid ball-rim bounce simulation.

Core Engineering Contributions

Multiplayer Match Flow

Engineered online matchmaking flows supporting two distinct rulesets: 15-minute full-court team games and high-tempo 3v3 half-court games with overtime sudden-death rules.

Touch Swipe Ball Physics

Authored trajectory prediction algorithms that translate finger swipe velocity, angle, and release timing into parabolic projectile impulses with backspin physics.

Android Profiling & Draw Calls

Profiled GPU fill rate on Vulkan/OpenGL ES mobile chipsets, reducing material texture samplers and optimizing court reflection shaders to maintain 60 FPS.

From Challenge to Playable Result

Mobile Swipe Arc Precision

Challenge: Slight variations in mobile touch screen digitizer sampling caused erratic shot arcs and unfair misses during quick swipe releases.

Solution: Implemented a weighted rolling average filter over the final 5 touch points before release, normalizing velocity spikes while preserving player intent.

Net & Rim Collision Physics

Challenge: Standard sphere collisions caused the basketball to clip through high-speed dunk animations or bounce unpredictably off the backboard.

Solution: Designed sub-stepped physics collision hulls for the rim ring and backboard, accompanied by procedural trigger zones for swish audio and net deformation.
