Basketball Mobile

An Android multiplayer arcade basketball title featuring full-court 5v5 matches, high-tempo 3v3 half-court street games, gesture-based touch swipe shooting, and Epic Online Services networking.

Project Overview

Basketball Mobile was developed to deliver responsive arcade sports gameplay on mobile touchscreens without sacrificing the depth of physics-driven shooting. The experience is designed around two distinct multiplayer formats: a structured 15-minute full-court game featuring two complete squads and a compact, fast-paced 3v3 half-court street match.

The project brings together basketball gameplay mechanics, touch swipe gesture calibration, Epic Online Services (EOS) for matchmaking and friend connectivity, and REST API integration for user authentication and leaderboard tracking.

My Technical Contributions

  • Multiplayer Match Architecture: Engineered online matchmaking flows supporting two distinct rulesets: 15-minute full-court team games and high-tempo 3v3 half-court games with overtime sudden-death rules.
  • Touch Swipe Shooting Physics: Authored trajectory calculation algorithms that translate finger swipe velocity, launch angle, and release timing into realistic parabolic projectile impulses with backspin physics.
  • Epic Online Services & API Integration: Integrated EOS for authentication, online lobbies, and match synchronization, combined with lightweight REST API calls for player stats and streak persistence.
  • Mobile Asset & GPU Profiling: Profiled GPU fill rates on Vulkan and OpenGL ES mobile chipsets, reducing draw calls, clamping texture samplers, and optimizing court reflection shaders to maintain a locked 60 FPS.

Challenges & Engineering Decisions

Mobile Swipe Arc Precision: Slight variations in mobile touch screen digitizer sampling rates caused erratic shot trajectories and frustrating misses during fast finger flick releases. Implemented a weighted rolling average filter over the final 5 touch coordinates before finger release, normalizing velocity spikes while preserving player intent.

Rim & Net Collision Physics: Standard spherical collision primitives caused high-velocity basketballs to clip through slam dunks or bounce unnaturally off the rim. Designed sub-stepped physics collision hulls for the rim ring and backboard, accompanied by procedural trigger zones for swish audio and net deformation.
